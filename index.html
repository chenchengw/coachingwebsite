Something Has to Change

The path you planned for looks nothing like it did a year ago. You're not sure if you need a new role, a new company, or a new chapter entirely — you just know something has to give.

We work on: separating burnout from misalignment, understanding what you actually want (not what you think you should want), and making the next move with conviction instead of panic.

The Rules Keep Changing

You're adapting as fast as you can — new skills, new expectations, new scoreboard. But the feedback doesn't match the effort, and nobody's telling you how you're being evaluated.

We work on: reading the real scoreboard, understanding what your level actually demands, and building a leadership style that's yours — not a performance.

The Room Got Quieter

The higher you've climbed, the lonelier it gets. You're navigating politics, managing up, and making high-stakes calls with less and less honest input.

We work on: thinking partnership, navigating power dynamics without losing yourself, and making decisions with clear eyes when nobody will tell you the truth.

Five roles. Five identity rewrites. Here's what I learned.

Over a decade at LinkedIn and Instacart, I was promoted five times. Each time, I had to become a different kind of leader:

  • IC to Tech Lead — the moment it stopped being about my code
  • Tech Lead to Manager — the scoreboard changed and nobody told me
  • Manager to Senior Manager — my job became about other people's success, and that was a grief I didn't expect
  • Senior Manager to Director — the politics weren't optional anymore. They were the job.
  • Director to Builder — I walked away from the title, joined an AI startup, and started building again.

Each of those transitions forced me to let go of something I was good at and become someone I didn't know how to be yet. I navigated all of them as often the only Asian and woman in the room — which meant there was less room to figure it out in public.

I coach from pattern recognition — not frameworks or clichés.
