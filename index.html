Something Has to Change

The path you planned for looks nothing like it did a year ago. You're not sure if you need a new role, a new company, or a new chapter entirely — you just know something has to give. Especially now, with AI rewriting which paths still exist and which were always borrowed.

What you walk away with: A path you actually want to take — and the conviction to take it.

The Rules Keep Changing

You're adapting as fast as you can — new skills, new expectations, new scoreboard. But the feedback doesn't match the effort, and nobody's telling you how you're being evaluated. Especially now, when AI is moving the scoreboard faster than anyone can document the new rules.

What you walk away with: A system thinker's read on the unwritten rules — and a presence the right people see at the right level.

The Room Got Quieter

For leaders running senior teams or building their own thing, the higher the stakes, the lonelier it gets. Your investors need your conviction. Your team needs your direction. Your clients need your steady hand. Your family needs you to be the rock at home. And you're making big calls with less and less honest input from anyone.

What you walk away with: A trusted thinking partner where you don't have to be the strong one — and the strategic muscle to make sharper calls in the rooms where you do.

I've been where you are.

Drawing from 10+ years at LinkedIn and Instacart — where I grew from IC to Engineering Director leading 50+ engineers — I bring an insider understanding of how performance, promotion, and influence actually work inside tech organizations. Each transition required becoming a different kind of leader:

  • IC to Tech Lead — it stopped being about my code
  • Tech Lead to Manager — the scoreboard changed and nobody told me
  • Manager to Senior Manager — my job became replacing myself
  • Senior Manager to Director — politics weren't optional anymore
  • Director to Builder — I walked away from the title and started building again

I navigated all of them as often the only Asian and woman in the room — which meant less room to figure it out in public.

I used this framework to navigate my five identity rewrites: you learn to see the game you're actually in, choose what you're playing for, and play your game with confidence. I help clients clarify what's really being asked, spot the patterns that are hard to see from the inside, and move forward with clarity and intention. That's the shape of the work.
